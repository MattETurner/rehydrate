reHydrate

An offline companion for the reMarkable.

Pulls your notebooks over USB and makes proper PDFs from your handwriting — all without sending your notes to anybody else's server.

Truly offline

reHydrate connects to your reMarkable through its USB cable. No Wi-Fi, no subscription, no servers in the middle. Your notes go from the tablet to your computer and stop there.

There's no reMarkable Connect needed and no monthly fee. The app works exactly the same with the tablet's cloud features turned off entirely.

Version history

Every time you sync, reHydrate keeps a snapshot of every notebook that changed. Decided you preferred last week's draft? It's still there — open the history view and restore. Want to see how a notebook looked a month ago? Same.

If you have more than one reMarkable, keep a separate library for each one and switch between them from the toolbar.

Beautiful PDFs

Notebooks open as proper PDFs — not blurry screenshots. Every pen, ballpoint, marker, fineliner, calligraphy stroke, and highlighter looks the way it does on the tablet. Sharp at any zoom, ready to print or share.

Privacy

reHydrate is built so your notes never leave your computer. Concretely:

  • No telemetry, no analytics, no "anonymous usage statistics". The app makes no network calls on launch.
  • Sync between your tablet and your computer happens entirely over the USB cable. The reMarkable's cloud is not in the loop.
  • Your tablet password is stored in your computer's secure password store (Keychain on macOS, the equivalent on Linux and Windows). Never in plain text on disk.
  • The only server the app ever contacts is your tablet, over USB.
  • An automated check runs on every release to make sure nobody accidentally adds a network call somewhere they shouldn't.

What it's not

  • It's not a cloud service. There's nothing to subscribe to.
  • It doesn't replace your reMarkable's tablet experience — it sits next to it, on your computer.
  • It's not yet officially signed for macOS or Windows, so the first launch needs an extra confirmation step (right-click → Open on macOS, "Run anyway" on Windows). Signing is on the roadmap.
